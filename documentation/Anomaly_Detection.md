# Anomaly Detection MethodologyThis document describes the statistical anomaly detection framework developed for the Insurance Claims Analytics project.---## ObjectiveThe objective of the anomaly detection layer is to:1. Identify statistically unusual insurance claims2. Detect claims with extreme values across key financial and medical metrics3. Support investigators in prioritizing claims for further review4. Provide an independent statistical layer separate from the risk scoring model---## Why Anomaly Detection?The risk scoring model classifies claims based on medical and financial risk.However, a claim may have:- Medium risk score- But extremely high Claim Ratio compared to the rest of the dataThis claim would not be flagged as High Risk by the risk model.But statistically, it is unusual.Anomaly detection captures these cases.---## Variables UsedFour variables were selected for anomaly detection:| Variable | Description ||---|---|| Claim Ratio | Claim Value ÷ Sum Insured || Cost Per Day | Hospital Bill ÷ Length of Stay || Claim Delay | Claim Date - Policy Start Date || Total Claim Amount | Total claim value |### Why These Variables?These variables were selected because:1. They represent key financial and operational aspects of claims2. Extreme values may indicate unusual patterns3. They are independent of the risk scoring model4. They are commonly used in claims analytics---## Method: Z-Score Analysis### What is a Z-Score?A Z-score measures how many standard deviations an observation is from the mean.Z = (X - Mean) / Standard Deviation### Interpretation| Z-Score | Interpretation ||---|---|| Between -2 and +2 | Within normal range || Between +2 and +3 | Moderately unusual || Greater than or equal to +3 | Strong statistical signal |### Threshold SelectionA threshold of **+3** was selected because:1. It is a standard threshold in statistical analysis2. Values beyond +3 standard deviations are rare (less than 0.3% in a normal distribution)3. It provides a strong signal without excessive false positives4. It is widely used in healthcare and insurance analytics---## Z-Score Features### 1. Claim Ratio Z-Score`Claim_Ratio_ZScore = (Claim_Ratio - Mean_Claim_Ratio) / StdDev_Claim_Ratio`Identifies claims with unusually high claim ratios.### 2. Cost Per Day Z-Score`Cost_Per_Day_ZScore = (Cost_Per_Day - Mean_Cost_Per_Day) / StdDev_Cost_Per_Day`Identifies claims with unusually high daily treatment costs.### 3. Claim Delay Z-Score`Claim_Delay_ZScore = (Claim_Delay - Mean_Claim_Delay) / StdDev_Claim_Delay`Identifies claims with unusually long delays between policy start and claim date.### 4. Total Claim Amount Z-Score`Total_Claim_Amount_ZScore = (Total_Claim_Amount - Mean_Total_Claim_Amount) / StdDev_Total_Claim_Amount`Identifies claims with unusually high total claim amounts.---## Anomaly Count### Definition`Anomaly_Count` is the number of Z-scores that exceed the +3 threshold.### CalculationAnomaly_Count =(IF Claim_Ratio_ZScore >= 3 THEN 1 ELSE 0) +(IF Cost_Per_Day_ZScore >= 3 THEN 1 ELSE 0) +(IF Claim_Delay_ZScore >= 3 THEN 1 ELSE 0) +(IF Total_Claim_Amount_ZScore >= 3 THEN 1 ELSE 0)### Range`Anomaly_Count` ranges from **0 to 4**.---## Potential Anomaly Classification### RuleA claim is classified as **Potential Anomaly** when:Anomaly_Count >= 2### Classification Table| Anomaly Count | Classification ||---|---:|| 0 | Normal || 1 | Normal || 2 | Potential Anomaly || 3 | Potential Anomaly || 4 | Potential Anomaly |---## Why Threshold = 2?| Reason | Explanation ||---|---|| One outlier could be a data error | A single extreme value may be due to data entry issues || Two or more outliers indicate a pattern | Multiple extreme values suggest a claim worth investigating || Reduces false positives | Fewer claims flagged for unnecessary review || Balances sensitivity and specificity | Captures truly unusual claims without overwhelming investigators |---## Separation from Risk Model### Important Distinction| Aspect | Risk Model | Anomaly Detection ||---|---|---|| What it measures | Medical and financial risk | Statistical unusualness || Output | Risk Level (Low/Medium/High) | Anomaly Classification (Normal/Anomaly) || Method | Weighted scoring framework | Z-score analysis || Purpose | Prioritize claims by risk | Detect statistically unusual claims |### Why Keep Them Separate?1. **A High Risk claim may not be statistically unusual**2. **A statistically unusual claim may not be High Risk**3. **Combining them provides a more complete picture**4. **Each serves a different purpose in the review process**---## Dashboard IntegrationThe anomaly detection results are displayed in the **Potential Anomalies & Outliers** page.Key fields displayed:- policy_id- Potential_Anomaly- Anomaly_Count- Claim_Risk_Level- Overall_Claim_Risk_Score- Claim_Ratio- Total_Claim_Amount- Cost_Per_Day- Claim_Delay- Medical_Risk_Score---## Interpretation### Normal (Anomaly_Count = 0 or 1)- No strong statistical signals detected- No immediate action required### Potential Anomaly (Anomaly_Count >= 2)- Two or more variables exceed the +3 Z-score threshold- Claim may warrant further investigation- Review recommended---## Important Note**Potential Anomaly does not mean confirmed fraud.**It indicates that the claim has unusual statistical characteristics that may warrant:- Additional review- Validation- Expert investigation---## Limitations1. The method assumes a normal distribution2. Some extreme values may be legitimate (e.g., complex medical cases)3. The threshold of +3 is arbitrary but statistically standard4. The method does not identify fraud directly5. Additional domain expertise is required for final decisions---## SummaryThe anomaly detection framework provides:- Statistical identification of unusual claims- An independent layer separate from risk scoring- A clear classification (Normal / Potential Anomaly)- Actionable insights for claims reviewThis framework supports efficient claims investigation by highlighting cases that are statistically unusual and may require further review.---**Last Updated:** September 2025
